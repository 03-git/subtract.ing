lookdown persona routing plan
==============================

goal: expand lookdown.tsv to catch intent from people who don't know
what they need yet. route by how someone *talks* about what they want,
not by what the software is called.

three lifecycle stages. same need, different language.

stage 1: aspirational child
----------------------------
doesn't know the tool exists. knows the person they want to be.
queries are about people, dreams, and "how did they."

  content creation / streaming:
    "i want to make youtube videos"
    "how do streamers put their face on the screen"
    "how did mr beast start"
    "i want to be a youtuber"
    "how do i record my screen"
    "how do i make a thumbnail"
    "how do people make those gaming videos"
    → OBS (streaming/recording), Kdenlive (editing), GIMP (thumbnails)

  music:
    "i want to make beats"
    "how do you make music on a computer"
    "how did [artist] make their songs"
    "i want to make a song"
    "what do music producers use"
    → LMMS (DAW), Audacity (recording/editing)

  art / design:
    "i want to draw on the computer"
    "how do people make digital art"
    "how do animators make cartoons"
    "i want to make a comic"
    → GIMP (raster), Inkscape (vector), Krita (painting), Pencil2D (animation)

  game making:
    "i want to make a game"
    "how do you make a video game"
    "how did minecraft get made"
    "i want to make a game like [x]"
    → Godot (engine), Tiled (level editor)

  sports / stats:
    "what are [player]'s stats"
    "who has the most home runs"
    "who won the [sport] last night"
    "what's [player]'s batting average"
    "who is the best [position] ever"
    → kiwix (wikipedia has career stats, records, history)
    → no software install needed. the corpus IS the tool.

  pop culture:
    "what order do i watch marvel movies"
    "when did [movie] come out"
    "who played [character]"
    "what's the best [genre] movie"
    "how many [franchise] movies are there"
    → kiwix (wikipedia covers filmography, discography, episode lists)
    → no software install needed.

  keyword patterns (child stage):
    "i want to be" / "i want to make" / "how did [person]"
    "how do people" / "how do you" / "how do streamers"
    "what do [professionals] use"
    indecision markers: "someday" / "when i grow up" / "is it hard to"


stage 2: economically motivated adult
--------------------------------------
knows what they need. can't or won't pay for it.
queries are about alternatives, cost, and "free."

  content creation:
    "free video editing software"
    "obs alternative" (already has obs, wants comparison)
    "video editor without subscription"
    "free screen recorder"
    "premiere pro alternative free"
    → Kdenlive, Shotcut, OBS

  office / productivity:
    "free word processor"
    "microsoft office alternative"
    "google docs but offline"
    "free spreadsheet"
    → LibreOffice

  design:
    "free photoshop"
    "photoshop alternative free"
    "free logo maker"
    "illustrator alternative"
    → GIMP, Inkscape

  audio:
    "free audio editor"
    "audacity alternative" (ironic — audacity IS the free one)
    "free podcast recording"
    "garageband for linux"
    → Audacity, Ardour

  accounting / business:
    "free accounting software"
    "quickbooks alternative free"
    "free invoicing"
    → GnuCash, Invoice Ninja

  media server:
    "free plex alternative"
    "media server free"
    "stream my own movies"
    → Jellyfin

  sports:
    "free sports stats api"
    "where to get box scores"
    "free fantasy football tools"
    → kiwix for reference, but live scores need network.
       lookdown could route to a local stats cache if seeded.

  keyword patterns (economic stage):
    "free" / "alternative" / "without subscription"
    "open source" (sometimes, but often just means free to them)
    "can't afford" / "too expensive" / "cheaper than"
    indecision markers: "is there a free" / "does anyone know a free"


stage 3: mature adult (choice, not economics)
----------------------------------------------
can afford anything. chooses open source on principle.
queries are about control, privacy, self-hosting, and quality.

  content creation:
    "open source video editor"
    "self-hosted streaming"
    "video editing that doesn't phone home"
    → Kdenlive, OBS, PeerTube (self-hosted streaming)

  office:
    "office suite that works offline"
    "libreoffice vs onlyoffice"
    "word processor with no cloud dependency"
    → LibreOffice

  design:
    "open source photo editor"
    "RAW editor linux"
    "vector editor that isn't adobe"
    → GIMP, darktable (RAW), Inkscape

  audio / music:
    "open source DAW"
    "pro audio on linux"
    "music production without ableton"
    → Ardour, Mixxx (DJ), Hydrogen (drums)

  media:
    "self-hosted media server"
    "jellyfin vs plex"
    "media server with no account required"
    → Jellyfin

  system:
    "open source firewall"
    "self-hosted dns"
    "privacy-respecting router firmware"
    → OPNsense/pfSense, Pi-hole/dnsmasq (you already need this), OpenWrt

  sports:
    "self-hosted sports tracker"
    "open source fitness tracking"
    → not really a category with good answers.
       the mature adult here uses a spreadsheet or a notebook.

  keyword patterns (mature stage):
    "self-hosted" / "open source" / "no telemetry"
    "no account required" / "works offline" / "no cloud"
    "privacy" / "sovereign" / "local first"
    indecision markers: rare. this person states what they want.


routing logic
-------------
these don't all become lookdown entries. three buckets:

  1. kiwix-routable (questions about people, stats, history, pop culture)
     → already handled IF the question ends in ? or starts with who/what/when
     → gap: "i want to be like mr beast" is not a question. it's aspirational.
        needs a lookdown entry that catches aspiration patterns and routes
        to a kiwix article about the person + a pointer to the tool.

  2. lookdown entries (direct software routing)
     → "free video editor" → echo the options + install command
     → "how do i record my screen" → echo "OBS. install: ..."
     → pattern: intent glob → brief answer + action

  3. skill files (procedural, multi-step)
     → "how do i start a youtube channel" is a skill, not a lookup
     → "how do i set up obs" is a skill
     → "how do i make a beat" is a skill
     → these go in ~/.subtract/skills/, not lookdown.tsv


cross-cutting patterns
----------------------
indecision detection:
  "should i" / "is it worth" / "is it hard" / "can i even"
  → these need a different tone. not "here is the tool."
     more like "yes, here's the first step." encouraging, not encyclopedic.
  → could be a tag: [encourage] that wraps the response differently.

lifecycle transitions:
  child → adult: "i've been using [tool], is there something better"
  adult → mature: "i want to own my data" / "tired of subscriptions"
  → the same person over time. the lookdown grows with them.

the software list (demand-driven, not shiny)
---------------------------------------------
each of these got popular because someone needed it:

  OBS         — streamers needed free broadcasting
  VLC         — played everything when nothing else would
  Blender     — artists who couldn't afford Maya
  Audacity    — people needed to edit audio
  GIMP        — couldn't afford photoshop
  Inkscape    — couldn't afford illustrator
  Krita       — digital painters wanted a real painting app
  LibreOffice — governments/schools couldn't afford MS Office
  Kdenlive    — video editing without premiere
  Godot       — game engine without unity's license changes
  FFmpeg      — the engine behind everything (OBS uses it)
  Jellyfin    — plex but without the account/paywall
  GnuCash     — quickbooks without quickbooks
  Ardour      — pro audio without pro tools
  darktable   — lightroom without adobe
  Kiwix       — wikipedia without internet
  dnsmasq     — dns without a vendor

  common thread: the commercial product created the need.
  the open source version answered it. demand, then supply.
  the shiny ones (cool-tech-demo, nobody-asked-for-this) don't last.


implementation order
--------------------
1. sports + pop culture: pure kiwix routing. no new software.
   just lookdown entries that catch the query shapes and forward to kiwix.

2. content creation (child): OBS + Kdenlive + GIMP.
   lookdown entries for aspiration patterns.
   skill files for "how to start."

3. content creation (adult): same tools, different entry points.
   "free [x] alternative" patterns.

4. music: LMMS + Audacity.
   aspiration patterns + skill files.

5. game making: Godot.
   aspiration patterns + skill files.

6. mature adult patterns: last, because this person
   already knows how to search. they find subtract,
   subtract doesn't need to find them.
